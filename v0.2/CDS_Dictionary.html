How this book worksEach section is one vocabulary — the answer list for one product question. The field key under each heading is the vocabulary's machine name; each entry shows the customer-facing Name and the code software stores.
Worked exampleA navy cushion: the vocabulary Colours (field key colour) answers "what colour is it?". Your PIM stores the code french_navy; the product page shows French Navy; the storefront filter shows its broad family Blue; a supplier writing "dark navy" matches automatically via the aliases. (The same example with every PIM and Shopify field name spelled out: Setup Runbook, Appendix A.)

Chapters

  1. Colours — Shades and the broad families they file under. A shade belongs to one or two families (first listed = primary).
  2. Materials — What things are made of - fibres and substances (cotton, wool, oak, ceramic). How a textile is constructed lives in Chapter 3.
  3. Fabrics & Construction — How a textile is made - weaves, knits and constructions (denim, jersey, tulle). Fibres such as cotton and wool live in Chapter 2, Materials: a cotton jersey tee is Material: Cotton + Fabric: Jersey.
  4. Care — Descriptive care values (deliberately not the trademarked care-symbol set).
  5. Sizing — Sizing systems, not sizes - a size value only means something with its system declared (AU 12, US bra, EU kids height).
  6. Apparel Attributes — Fit, style, occasion, pattern, neckline, sleeve and length vocabularies. The style vocabulary is shared across fashion and interiors.
  7. Homewares Attributes — Rooms, surface finishes and shapes.
  8. Footwear — Styles, heel heights, closures, toe shapes and width fittings. Upper materials reuse Chapter 2.
  9. Jewellery & Accessories — Metals, stones, closures and piece styles.
  10. Beauty & Personal Care — Skin and hair states, scent families, formulations and finishes. Benefit claims (anti-ageing, hypoallergenic) are excluded by design - they need evidence, not filters.
  11. Electronics & Appliances — Brown and white goods: connections, screen technologies, power, mounting and energy-label schemes. Compliance marks and vendor ecosystems excluded.
  12. Sports & Outdoor — The shop-by-sport facet, plus surf wetsuit attributes (style, thickness, zip entry, seam construction). Performance claims, skill levels and vendor fin systems excluded.
  13. Toys & Games — Play types and retail age bands. The packaging safety mark (3+) is a compliance label, not filter vocabulary.
  14. Pet Supplies — Pet types, species-neutral life stages (puppy/kitten are display mappings) and animal sizes. Breed lists and health claims excluded.
  15. Furniture — Assembly forms. Furniture styles live in the Chapter 6 style vocabulary; upholstery reuses Fabrics and Materials.
  16. Garden & Outdoor — Sun exposure - a grower-declared growing condition. Plant taxonomies and watering claims deliberately excluded.
